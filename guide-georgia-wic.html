Georgia WIC

Georgia WIC offers food and nutrition support for pregnancy and early childhood. Begin through Georgia Gateway or a local WIC clinic, then complete the clinic’s nutrition assessment.

Who can apply

Georgia WIC serves eligible low-income pregnant, postpartum or breastfeeding applicants and children younger than five who are at nutritional risk. The clinic assesses eligibility; an online assessment is only a preliminary check.

What help is available

Benefits include selected foods on an eWIC card, nutrition information, breastfeeding support and referrals to healthcare or community services.

Documents to prepare

  • Ask the local clinic for its current document checklist.
  • Prepare identity, residence and household-income records.
  • Have available pregnancy, child-health or existing-benefit information ready for the appointment.

How to get started

  1. Start the application

    Choose WIC at Georgia Gateway or contact a clinic through the state WIC directory.

  2. Arrange the appointment

    The clinic follows up about assessment and benefits. Confirm who needs to attend and which documents it needs.

  3. Complete certification

    Discuss the applicant’s nutrition needs and ask staff to explain the eWIC card and approved foods.

After applying

Keep clinic appointments and use its instructions for benefit changes or card problems. Ask for breastfeeding assistance when needed.

Common questions

Does an online assessment enroll me?

No. It indicates possible eligibility; the clinic completes the assessment and certification.

Where can I use the food benefits?

Use the eWIC card with WIC-authorized vendors and the program’s approved-food guidance.

Can I start through Gateway?

Yes. Georgia’s WIC page links Gateway for applications and a clinic locator for appointment help.
