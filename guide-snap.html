SNAP food benefits

SNAP is a federal food assistance program run by state agencies. If approved, eligible households receive monthly funds on an EBT card for groceries.

What it is
Monthly money on an EBT card for groceries
Who runs it
Your state, with federal funding
How to apply
Online, by phone or in person
How long it takes
Usually up to 30 days; 7 days in an emergency

Ready to apply?

Your state agency handles SNAP applications. This guide explains the process and links to state agency information.

In Arizona, SNAP is called Nutrition Assistance

It is run by the Arizona Department of Economic Security. Program names and rules vary by state, so check your state's page for local details.

Who can apply

SNAP looks at your household: the people who live together and buy and make food together. You may qualify if your household's income is under your state's limit, which is usually based on the federal poverty level and the number of people in your home.

Older adults and people with disabilities have extra rules that often make it easier to qualify, like counting medical costs. Some adults without children have to meet work rules to keep benefits for more than a few months.

What you can get

The amount depends on your income and household size. Households with less money get more. Benefits load onto an EBT card each month, which works like a debit card at most grocery stores, many farmers markets and some online stores.

Documents to have ready

You can apply before you have everything. The agency will tell you what else they need.

  • A photo ID for the person applying
  • Proof of where you live, like a lease or a utility bill
  • Pay stubs from the last 30 days, or a letter about other income
  • Rent or mortgage and utility bills
  • Social Security numbers for people applying
  • Child care or medical bills, if you pay them

How to apply

  1. Apply online, by phone or in person

    Use your state's agency website, call the agency or visit a local office. You can turn in a short form with just your name, address and signature to lock in your start date.

  2. Complete an interview

    Someone from the agency calls or meets with you, usually within a couple of weeks. They go over your application and ask about income and costs.

  3. Send any missing documents

    If they need proof of something, they send a list with a due date. Send it by that date so your application isn't closed.

  4. Get your decision and EBT card

    Most households get a decision within 30 days. If approved, your EBT card arrives by mail and benefits load each month.

After you apply

Keep your phone on and check your mail, since the agency will contact you about the interview. If you're approved, you'll need to renew, usually every 6 to 12 months, and report some changes, like a new job.

If you're denied or get less than you expected, you have the right to ask for a fair hearing. The decision letter explains how and the deadline to ask.

Common questions

Can I get SNAP if I work?

Yes. Many working households qualify. What matters is your household size, income and some costs, not whether you have a job.

Do I need to be a U.S. citizen?

Citizens and many lawfully present immigrants can get SNAP. Family members who don't qualify can still apply for others in the home, like children.

What can I buy with SNAP?

Most food for your household, including fruit, vegetables, meat, dairy, bread and seeds to grow food. You can't buy alcohol, tobacco, vitamins, hot prepared food or household supplies.

What if I need food right away?

Tell the agency. Households with very little money may get benefits within 7 days. Call 211 for a food bank in the meantime.

Apply on your state's agency website

Applying is free. You should never pay anyone to apply for SNAP.
