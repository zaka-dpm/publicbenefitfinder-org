Assistance programs in District of Columbia

Explore public benefits and assistance resources by the kind of help you need. Agencies decide eligibility and availability.

Find programs for your situation

Food assistance

  • DC SNAP (Food Assistance)

    DC SNAP supplies food benefits through an EBT card to eligible households. Apply through District Direct or DHS paper application routes.

    Read the application guide

  • DC WIC

    DC WIC provides food and nutrition support for eligible pregnancy, postpartum and early-childhood needs. A clinic appointment completes the application process.

    Read the application guide

Housing assistance

  • Legal Aid DC

    Legal Aid DC offers free help for eligible people with legal matters in the District. Apply online, by phone or through a listed intake location.

    Read the application guide

Utility assistance

  • DC Energy Assistance (LIHEAP & Utility Discount Program)

    DC offers energy-bill assistance through LIHEAP and discounts through UDP. DOEE announces FY27 applications opening October 20, 2026.

    Read the application guide

  • Weatherization Assistance Program (DC)

    DC weatherization helps qualifying homes become more energy efficient. DOEE’s current page reports delayed WAP openings, so confirm availability before applying.

    Read the application guide

Health coverage

  • DC Medicaid

    DC Medicaid pays for covered health services for eligible residents. District Direct and DHS service centers provide application routes.

    Read the application guide

  • DC Health Care Alliance

    DC Health Care Alliance is locally funded medical assistance for qualifying residents outside Medicaid. Current adult eligibility differs from older descriptions.

    Read the application guide

Cash and income support

  • DC Earned Income Tax Credit

    DC’s earned income tax credit is a refundable tax credit for eligible workers. Claim it through the District’s individual income-tax return.

    Read the application guide

  • DC TANF (Cash Assistance)

    DC TANF provides cash and supportive services for eligible families. DHS reviews family circumstances and financial eligibility.

    Read the application guide

  • Unemployment Insurance (DC DOES)

    DC unemployment insurance provides temporary support for qualifying workers. DOES lists initial claims, weekly certifications and account services separately.

    Read the application guide

Additional support

  • 211 Warmline (District of Columbia)

    DC’s 211 Warmline helps residents find social-service resources. It is a referral line for non-emergency needs.

    Read the application guide

Program names come from our existing directory. Funding and rules can change; confirm details with the administering agency before applying.
