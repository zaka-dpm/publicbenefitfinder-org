2-1-1 in Georgia (United Way Network)

Georgia’s 211 service connects callers with community resources for food, housing, utilities, healthcare and other needs. The United Ways of Georgia directory identifies regional contacts.

Who can apply

The helpline is free and confidential. A referral is available without a benefit approval; each receiving program applies its own service-area, income and availability rules.

What help is available

A specialist helps locate relevant organizations. The state network advertises phone access around the clock, while individual referred agencies have their own hours.

Documents to prepare

  • Know your county or ZIP code.
  • Explain the need and any urgent date, such as a scheduled utility disconnection.
  • Record the referred provider’s name, contact and intake instructions.

How to get started

  1. Call 211

    Use the Georgia regional directory if you need to identify the center serving your area.

  2. Request a specific referral

    Tell the specialist whether you need food today, help with an energy bill, shelter or another service.

  3. Contact the agency

    Ask whether intake is open, what you should bring and whether the agency serves your address.

After applying

If a provider cannot help, explain why when asking 211 for alternatives. Call emergency services for an immediate threat to safety.

Common questions

Does 211 provide a cash award?

211 connects you to providers; it does not replace their benefit applications.

What if I do not know the program name?

Describe the problem and your location. The specialist can help identify relevant services.

Do referral agencies all operate 24 hours?

No. The network advertises round-the-clock helpline access, but referred providers set their own hours.
